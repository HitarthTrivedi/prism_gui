# Prism — Presentation Script

A slide-by-slide script. Each slide has three parts:

- **On the slide** — the few words that go on the screen.
- **Say** — what the presenter says, in plain words. About 45–75 seconds per slide.
- **Visual** — what picture or screenshot to show.

22 slides, roughly 20–25 minutes with questions.

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**On the slide**
Prism
Your AI tools, working together, on your own computer.

**Say**
Good morning. Today I want to show you Prism. Prism is a small program that runs on your laptop. You tell it a job in your own words. It works out the steps, opens the AI tools you already use, does the job in them one by one, and brings everything back to one place. Nothing of yours leaves your machine unless you say so. That is the whole idea, and the next twenty minutes are about what that means for your business.

The Prism home screen.

## Slide 2 — The problem

**On the slide**
AI is powerful. Using it is tiring.
- Ten tabs open
- Copy from one, paste into the next
- Which tool for which job?
- Where did the answer go?

**Say**
Most of us already have ChatGPT, maybe Claude, Perplexity, Canva. They are all good at different things. The trouble is not the tools. The trouble is the running around. You write a prompt in one tab, copy the answer, paste it into another, fix it, paste it into a third. By the fourth tool you have forgotten what the first one said. Small businesses do not have a person to do this all day. So the tools sit there, paid for and half used.

## Slide 3 — What Prism is, in one line

**On the slide**
Describe the job → Prism plans it → your tools do it → results in one place.

**Say**
Prism is the person who does the running around. You type, or say, the job: "write a quotation for this inquiry" or "make a short video about our new product." Prism works out the stages and picks a tool for each one. Then it opens your Chrome, signs in as you, does each stage, and collects the results. You review the plan before it runs and you see everything after. That is it. No new AI to learn. No new subscription.

**Visual**
A simple four-box flow: Describe, Plan, Run, Results.

## Slide 4 — Your tools, your logins

**On the slide**
No new subscription.
- Uses ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Canva, Apollo and 30 more
- Opens them in your own Chrome, as you
- Free plans work. Paid plans work better.

**Say**
This is the part people find hard to believe. Prism does not have its own AI. It drives the tools you already pay for, inside your own Chrome, with your own login. If you have the free ChatGPT, Prism uses the free ChatGPT. If you upgrade, Prism gets the upgrade. There are no per-word bills hiding anywhere. The only account Prism itself needs is a free key from a service called Groq, which it uses to plan the steps. That takes two minutes to set up.

## Slide 5 — How a job runs

**On the slide**
1. Describe — say it in your own words
2. Plan — Prism picks the stages and the tools
3. Review — drop a step, or send it elsewhere
4. Run — your Chrome opens and works, in order
5. Results — everything kept in History

**Say**
Let me walk through one job. I type: "find ten plastic moulding companies in Gujarat and draft an introduction email." Prism reads that and shows me a plan: research with Perplexity, find contacts with Apollo, write the email with Claude. I can see it before anything happens. If I don't want the research step, I remove it. Then I press Start. Chrome opens, each tool gets its prompt, and Prism waits for the real answer, not a half answer. At the end I have the list, the contacts and the email in one screen. And it is saved, so next week I can open it again.

## Slide 6 — Your files never leave your machine

**On the slide**
Confidential by design.
- Drawings, Gerber files and 3D models are measured on your computer
- Only the numbers go to an AI, never the file
- No Prism server ever sees your work

**Say**
For a manufacturer this is the most important slide. Your customer's drawing is your customer's product. Prism never uploads it to any AI. When it needs to measure a drawing, a PCB file or a 3D model, it does the measuring itself, on your laptop, with engineering libraries. Only the measured numbers, like "board is 100 by 80 millimetres," are ever shown to a tool. And Prism has no cloud of its own. The only thing it talks to on our side is a licence check, which sends a licence number and nothing else.

## Slide 8 — Email automation

**On the slide**
Every inquiry logged, quoted and chased.
- Reads your mailboxes
- Registers each inquiry in one shared Excel file
- Drafts the quotation from your rate list
- Reminds the customer, and checks the PO when it arrives

**Say**
Most of a small factory's business happens over email, and inquiries get lost. Prism reads your mailbox, finds the inquiries, and writes each one into a register that opens in Excel. It gives every inquiry a number and a folder. It drafts the quotation using your own rate list and your own template. If the customer goes quiet, it reminds you, or them. When the purchase order comes, it reads it and checks it against the quote. The register stays your file, in your folder. If you stop using Prism tomorrow, you still have it.

## Slide 9 — BOQ and BOM

**On the slide**
Quantities off a drawing. Parts list off a drawing.
- Attach a DXF or PDF, or just describe the job
- Prism counts and measures, on your computer
- A checkable CSV first, then the written document
- You price it

**Say**
A Bill of Quantities is what a contractor needs to price a job: how many metres of cable, how many cameras, how many fittings. A Bill of Materials is the same idea for a fabricated machine: the parts to make it. Attach the drawing and Prism measures it here, then saves every figure to a CSV so you can check it. Only then does it hand the numbers to your writing tool to lay out the document, with your own template if you have one. Prism never invents a quantity. If it could not measure something, it says so.

## Slide 10 — Gerber

The five numbers a PCB fab asks for.
- Board size, track width, track spacing, drill size, hole count
- Drop the customer's zip exactly as it arrived
- Fill the client's own quotation form
- The Gerber files are never shown to an AI

**Say**
If you make printed circuit boards, every inquiry starts with the same five questions, and someone spends an hour opening layers to answer them. Prism reads the Gerber files and measures those five numbers directly. It can fill them into your customer's own Excel form. It can clean everything outside the board outline and save the cleaned files. And the design never goes near an AI. Only the numbers do, if you ask for a written reply.

## Slide 11 — STEP

**On the slide**
A 3D model, measured and drawn.
- Every part's size, thickness, holes and weight
- A dimensioned drawing sheet: front, top and side views, in millimetres
- Then Draft, Ask or Edit
- The model never leaves your machine

**Say**
A customer sends a 3D model of an enclosure or a plastic part. Prism opens it, measures each part's size, sheet or wall thickness, every hole by diameter, and the weight in steel or plastic. Then it draws a proper drawing sheet itself: front, top and side views with dimension lines, a hole table and a title block, in about a second. From there you choose. Draft asks ChatGPT for a styled version of the sheet. Ask gets suggestions for the part, for example how to make it lighter. Edit applies agreed changes to a copy of the model and measures it again. The original file is never touched.

**On the slide**
"Anything to change?"

Say "make the images brighter" or "use the new price list I'm attaching," and Prism figures out which step that belongs to and re-runs just that step, in the same conversation, so the tool remembers everything it already knew.

## Slide 21 — What Prism is not

**On the slide**
Honest limits.
- Needs the internet and your Chrome open
- Tools change their websites; we update within a day
- Free plans have limits; the paid ones are yours, not ours
- It does not replace your judgement. It saves you the running around.

**Say**
Three honest things. Prism needs the internet, because the tools are online. When ChatGPT or Apollo redesign their pages, a step can fail until we push an update, usually within a day, and your work is never lost. Free plans have monthly limits, and those are set by the tool, not by us. And Prism does not decide for you. You see the plan before it runs and the result after. It saves the hours, not the thinking.

## Appendix — Quick answers for Q&A

- **Does Prism store our data?** No. Settings live in one file on your computer. Registers and outputs are files in folders you choose.
- **Which AI does it use?** Whichever you have. Planning uses Groq, which is free. Everything else runs in your own Chrome.
- **Can two people use it?** Yes, one seat per computer. The inquiry register can be shared on a network folder.
- **What if a tool is down?** The step is marked, the rest of the run continues, and the link to that tool's tab is kept.
- **Windows or Mac?** Both, plus Linux.
- **What does it cost?** A yearly licence per seat, with add-ons priced separately. No usage bills.
